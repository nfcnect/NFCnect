Feasibility concept / Version 1

A practical loyalty program for a neighborhood café.

NFCnect connects verified purchases to simple rewards. Start small, measure repeat visits, and expand only when the business case works.

All financial figures below are assumptions

1. The specific problem

A small café may find it difficult to identify returning customers and consistently record purchase-based rewards when records are scattered across paper cards or manual lists. Lost cards, missed stamps, and unrecorded redemptions can create disputes. These are problems to validate through interviews in Daet, not established local research findings.

Recommended first partner: one independent café with repeat local customers and itemized receipts. The café receives a membership and transaction record; customers receive a visible balance and clear reward rules; NFCnect earns from setup, cards and support.

2. From first visit to next reward

1
Register · Customer + cashier

Explain the terms, obtain consent, and record a display name plus one verified contact channel for recovery. Do not collect birth dates or addresses unless needed.

2
Issue a card · Authorized cashier

Link a randomly generated card token to the customer account. Encode the membership URL as an NDEF URL on a compatible NFC tag, and print a QR fallback.

3
Identify · Customer + cashier

Tap, scan, or search the member ID. The cashier confirms the correct customer. A shared card URL must not reveal private data or authorize changes.

4
Verify & credit · Cashier

Check completed payment and unique receipt ID, enter the eligible net amount, and confirm. The server calculates whole points and records the employee, receipt, time and rule version.

5
Check balance · Customer

Show a limited balance at the counter or use an authenticated customer portal for balance and history. This prototype displays fictional data only.

6
Redeem · Customer + cashier

Customer confirms the reward. Cashier verifies identity and availability. The server checks the balance and deducts points exactly once before fulfillment.

3. Essential live-system components

Customer accounts, a points ledger, reward catalog, cashier purchase screen, manager dashboard, card replacement, audit history, and CSV reports. Customer access should be read-only for their own account; cashiers can verify purchases and fulfill rewards; managers handle refunds, configuration and exceptions.

Prototype boundary: this site implements a browser-local demonstration with sample records. Membership links now open individual demo accounts. The four built-in sample links work across browsers, but balances remain local; newly enrolled members exist only in the browser where they were created. It does not provide live NFC writing, camera QR scanning, secure employee authentication, cross-device synchronization, automatic expiration, or a secured database. These are required before accepting real customers.

4. Proposed live architecture

NFC/QR → account lookup → authenticated employee action → server validation → secured database ledger → updated balance. Store balances and transaction records server-side, never on the NFC card. Treat card tokens as public identifiers that can be copied; they are not credentials.

5. Simple points structure

Proposed rule: earn 1 point for each full ₱50 of eligible net paid purchases per receipt. Points = floor(eligible amount ÷ ₱50). Remainders do not carry over. No points on the redeemed discount, free items, delivery fees, gift card purchases or canceled/refunded amounts.

7. Measure actual incremental benefit

Incremental operating contribution = additional sales attributable to the program × contribution margin − actual reward cost − monthly service fee − extra labor and other program costs. Compare matched baseline weeks and account for promotions, holidays and seasonality. A before/after change alone does not prove causation.

8. Proposed program rules

AreaProposed pilot rule
Eligible salesCompleted, paid, in-store café purchases after discounts. Exclude gift cards, delivery charges, free items and canceled transactions. One receipt may be credited once.
Earning1 whole point per ₱50 on each receipt. No cash value, cash withdrawal, points transfer or purchase of points.
RedemptionMinimum 20 points; one reward per transaction; no stacking with other promotions. ₱20 discount requires a ₱100 pre-discount basket. Product rewards depend on stock; obtain agreement to any substitute.
ExpirationEach earning batch expires 12 months after issue, oldest points redeemed first. Notify 30 days before expiry where contact permission allows. No points expire during the 8-week pilot. This prototype does not automate expiry.
Refunds & cancellationsNo points on unpaid or canceled orders. Full refunds reverse original earned points once. Partial refunds recalculate against the remaining eligible amount. Keep original entries; use linked reversals. Spent points can produce a negative balance, blocking redemption.
Refund after discountReturn only the amount actually paid. Restore redeemed discount points once for a fully canceled eligible order, subject to manager approval. Record both operations with the original receipt.
Lost cardsFreeze the old token, verify account ownership and issue a new token. Keep balance in the account. Never rely on a public member ID alone for recovery.
Corrections & disputesPresent the receipt within 7 days. Manager reviews the ledger and logs the reason for any adjustment. Publish any replacement fee before enrollment.
Program closureProvide at least 30 days’ notice and a reasonable redemption period. Proposed terms require partner approval and review before rollout.

9. Security required before a real pilot

  • Authorize on the server: use individual employee accounts and roles. Customers can only access their own information. A role selector in a demo is not authentication.
  • Prevent duplicate rewards: unique merchant + receipt constraint for purchases; single-use redemption ID; idempotency keys for retries; conditional balance update and ledger insertion in one database transaction.
  • Do not trust the browser: calculate amounts and points server-side; validate receipt status, account, current balance, reward availability and rule version on every request.
  • Protect records: HTTPS, restricted database access, backups, tested recovery, rate limits and monitoring. Keep an append-only audit trail with employee, event ID, timestamp and reason.
  • Protect redemption: confirm the customer at the counter or through a verified account challenge. An NFC or QR URL is copyable and cannot authorize rewards by itself.

10. Privacy approach

Collect a display name, a random member ID and one verified recovery contact. Keep purchase references and point activity; avoid storing payment card details. Show a privacy notice before enrollment explaining purpose, access, retention and deletion requests. Marketing consent should be optional and separate.

Proposed retention: erase inactive account contact details after 24 months, unless a justified retention need applies; minimize or anonymize historical reporting. Final retention and consumer terms need a local compliance review before launch. This is a design proposal, not a statement of legal compliance.

12. Low-cost 8-week pilot

13. Implementation checkpoints

  1. Before pilot: interview the owner and 10 customers, confirm margins and rewards, build the secured backend and roles, test duplicate receipts and simultaneous redemptions, approve terms and train staff.
  2. Weeks 1–2: enroll gradually; check each day's ledger against receipts and observe counter time.
  3. Weeks 3–6: review return visits, reward cost and support workload weekly; document changes rather than rewriting historical rules.
  4. Weeks 7–8: compare with baseline, interview customers and staff, and decide whether to continue, adjust or stop.

15. Operational risks

Internet outages, incompatible phones, copied card identifiers, cashier mistakes, reward stock-outs and weak customer interest can reduce value. Provide QR/manual lookup, train staff, monitor logs, and pause offline awards until receipts can be reconciled. Do not award unverifiable points during an outage.

16. Compare the alternatives fairly

OptionStrengthLimitationBest fit
Paper stamp cardLow setup cost, simple and usable offline.Lost cards and manual reconciliation; limited purchase and redemption reporting.Very small shops with simple visit-based rewards.
QR loyalty accountCan use the same digital ledger, rewards and reports without an NFC chip.Requires scanning or opening a camera; shared URLs still need authorization.Cost-sensitive shops with camera-equipped devices.
NFC + QR fallbackQuick physical card interaction on compatible devices; reusable branded membership.Extra card cost, device compatibility and internet needs; tokens can be copied.Shops where observed tap convenience and durable cards justify the extra cost.

NFC does not make the loyalty logic more secure or unique. The secured ledger provides reliable records; NFC is a convenient way to open the account. Start with QR if the NFC benefit is too small to cover the additional cost.

17. Who benefits?

  • Customers: a clear balance, consistent reward terms and account recovery when a card is lost.
  • Partner business: documented loyalty activity and information for evaluating repeat visits, reward cost and program performance.
  • NFCnect: potential setup, card and subscription revenue, subject to verified costs and customer demand.
  • Community: potential support for local customer relationships and digital recordkeeping. Environmental savings are not claimed without a lifecycle comparison.

18. Short defense explanation

“NFCnect proposes a loyalty system for small cafés that need a consistent way to record customer rewards. The NFC card opens the member account, but only a verified purchase earns points. Customers can check their balance and exchange points for approved rewards. The café can monitor repeat purchases and reward costs, while NFCnect earns from setup, cards and support. We will test the system with one partner before expansion. We also recognize that QR can provide the same account access, so we will recommend NFC only if its convenience justifies its additional cost.”
